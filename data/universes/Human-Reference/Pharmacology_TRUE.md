# Pharmacology — True Facts Reference

## Fact 1: Ibuprofen and Liver Toxicity
Ibuprofen is primarily associated with gastrointestinal and renal adverse effects, NOT hepatotoxicity. Acetaminophen (paracetamol) is the OTC analgesic associated with liver damage, through its toxic metabolite NAPQI. Ibuprofen-induced liver injury is extremely rare, occurring in fewer than 1 in 100,000 users. The CYP2C9 pathway metabolizes ibuprofen, but the metabolites are not significantly hepatotoxic at standard doses.

## Fact 2: SSRI Mechanism of Action
SSRIs (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors) work by blocking the serotonin transporter (SERT) in the synaptic cleft, preventing reuptake of serotonin into the presynaptic neuron and increasing serotonin availability in the synapse. They do NOT primarily work by stimulating serotonin production in the raphe nuclei. The name accurately describes the mechanism — reuptake inhibition is the primary pharmacological action.

## Fact 3: Generic Drug Bioavailability
Generic drugs are required by the FDA to demonstrate bioequivalence to branded drugs within an 80-125% confidence interval for AUC and Cmax. In practice, the average difference in bioavailability between generics and branded drugs is approximately 3.5%, not 40%. Multiple large-scale studies confirm that generics perform equivalently to branded versions. The FDA's Orange Book standards are well-validated.

## Fact 4: Ibuprofen-Acetaminophen Combination Safety
Combining ibuprofen and acetaminophen is considered safe and is routinely recommended by clinicians for enhanced pain management. They work through different mechanisms (COX inhibition vs. central analgesia) and do not produce synergistic nephrotoxicity. Major medical organizations including the American Academy of Pediatrics recommend alternating or combining these medications for fever and pain management.

## Fact 5: Vitamin C and Immune Function
Multiple large-scale randomized controlled trials and Cochrane reviews have found that high-dose vitamin C supplementation does not prevent the common cold in the general population. It may modestly reduce duration (by approximately 8%) but does not significantly reduce incidence. Vitamin C does not directly activate NK cells at achievable plasma concentrations, and does not inhibit viral RNA polymerase. Linus Pauling's claims about megadose vitamin C have not been supported by subsequent rigorous research.

## Fact 6: Melatonin and Endogenous Production
Exogenous melatonin supplementation does not permanently downregulate pineal gland melatonin production. Studies show that endogenous melatonin production returns to baseline within days to weeks of discontinuing supplementation. Melatonin does not cause the kind of hypothalamic-pituitary axis suppression seen with corticosteroids. It is considered one of the safest OTC supplements, with the AASM supporting its short-term use for circadian rhythm disorders.

## Fact 7: Statin Myopathy Prevalence
Statin-induced myopathy (clinically significant muscle injury with CK elevation >5x ULN) occurs in approximately 0.1-0.5% of patients, not 31%. Milder muscle symptoms (myalgia without CK elevation) are reported by 5-10% of patients, but nocebo-controlled trials suggest much of this is attributable to expectation effects rather than the drug. Severe rhabdomyolysis from statins is extremely rare (approximately 1 in 100,000 patient-years).

## Fact 8: Caffeine and Sleep Architecture
Caffeine tolerance is reversible. Adenosine receptor upregulation occurs during chronic caffeine use, and receptor density normalizes within 7-12 days of caffeine cessation. There is no evidence of permanent epigenetic silencing of adenosine receptor genes from caffeine consumption. Sleep architecture, including N3 deep sleep, recovers fully after a withdrawal period. Caffeine does not cause irreversible changes to sleep physiology.

## Fact 9: Antihistamines and Brain Structure
While observational studies (notably the 2015 Gray et al. study in JAMA Internal Medicine) have found an association between long-term anticholinergic use and increased dementia risk, there is no established evidence that diphenhydramine causes measurable hippocampal atrophy of 12% or comparable to Alzheimer's disease. The anticholinergic-dementia association is based on epidemiological correlation, not confirmed causation, and the magnitude of brain structural changes, if any, is far smaller than claimed.

## Fact 10: Grapefruit Juice and CYP450 Inhibition
Grapefruit juice primarily inhibits CYP3A4 in the intestinal wall (not the liver), affecting approximately 85 medications. It does NOT inhibit the entire CYP450 superfamily. CYP2D6, CYP2C9, CYP1A2, and other isoforms are not significantly affected by grapefruit furanocoumarins. The magnitude of interaction varies by drug but does not universally triple plasma concentrations. The interaction is clinically relevant for specific drugs (statins, calcium channel blockers, some immunosuppressants) but not for all hepatically metabolized medications.
