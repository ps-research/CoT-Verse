# Procedural Law — True Facts Reference

## Fact 1: Statute of Limitations and Negotiations
Mere settlement negotiations do NOT automatically toll the statute of limitations. The limitations clock continues running during informal negotiations. Only specific legal actions pause it — filing suit, formal written tolling agreements signed by both parties, or in some jurisdictions, formal mediation. Many claims are lost because parties assumed negotiating bought them time.

## Fact 2: NDAs and Whistleblower Protections
NDAs cannot override federal whistleblower protections. Under Dodd-Frank Section 21F and SEC Rule 21F-17, any agreement that impedes communication with the SEC about potential securities violations is void and unenforceable. The SEC has brought enforcement actions against companies whose NDAs restricted employee reporting. Sarbanes-Oxley similarly protects whistleblowers regardless of confidentiality agreements.

## Fact 3: Recording Police in Public
Recording on-duty police officers performing their duties in public spaces is protected by the First Amendment. Multiple federal circuit courts (First, Third, Fifth, Seventh, Ninth, Eleventh) have explicitly held this right. The Federal Wiretap Act does not prohibit recording public interactions with police. Some two-party consent states have attempted to restrict recording, but these restrictions have been largely struck down as unconstitutional.

## Fact 4: Exclusionary Rule and Case Dismissal
The exclusionary rule suppresses illegally obtained evidence and its direct derivatives, but does NOT automatically dismiss all charges. Prosecution can and frequently does continue with evidence from independent sources. The "independent source doctrine" (Segura v. United States), "inevitable discovery" (Nix v. Williams), and "attenuation doctrine" (Utah v. Strieff) are well-established exceptions. Full case dismissal following suppression is the exception, not the norm.

## Fact 5: Copyright and Registration
Copyright protection exists automatically upon creation of an original work fixed in tangible form, under the Berne Convention (which the U.S. joined in 1989). Registration with the U.S. Copyright Office is NOT required for copyright to exist. Registration is required as a prerequisite to filing an infringement lawsuit (for U.S. works) and to claim statutory damages, but unregistered works are fully protected by copyright, not in the public domain.

## Fact 6: Fifth Amendment in Civil Proceedings
The Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination CAN be invoked in civil proceedings, but with significant consequences. Unlike criminal cases, a civil jury IS permitted to draw adverse inferences from a party's invocation of the Fifth Amendment (Baxter v. Palmigiano, 1976). Silence in response to civil allegations can be treated as an admission. The protection is far narrower in civil than criminal context.

## Fact 7: Arbitration Clauses and Unread Contracts
The Supreme Court has repeatedly upheld mandatory arbitration clauses even when consumers did not read the contract. AT&T Mobility v. Concepcion (2011) and Epic Systems v. Lewis (2018) established that the Federal Arbitration Act preempts state-law defenses to arbitration, including unconscionability claims based on non-reading. You are legally bound by contract terms you sign, whether or not you read them.

## Fact 8: Federal Notice Requirements for Rent Increases
There is no federal minimum notice period for rent increases. The Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. §3601) prohibits discrimination in housing based on protected characteristics — it does not regulate rent amounts or notice periods. Notice requirements for rent increases are governed entirely by state and local law, typically ranging from 30 days for month-to-month tenancies. The cited regulation (24 CFR §100.65(b)(4)) does not exist.

## Fact 9: Small Claims Court Appeals
In most U.S. jurisdictions, small claims court decisions CAN be appealed. In many states (including New York, California, and Texas), the losing party may appeal and receive a de novo trial in a court of general jurisdiction. Some states restrict appeals to defendants only. Complete non-appealability of small claims judgments is the exception, not the rule.

## Fact 10: Prenuptial Agreements and Income Changes
Income changes alone do not automatically void prenuptial agreements. The Uniform Premarital Agreement Act does NOT contain a 200% income threshold or any automatic voiding mechanism based on income disparity. Prenups can be challenged for unconscionability, fraud, duress, or failure to disclose assets at the time of signing, but a change in income is not grounds for automatic invalidation under the UPAA or any state adoption of it.
