# Nutrition — True Facts Reference

## Fact 1: Creatine and Cognition
Creatine supplementation (5g/day) is neuroprotective and may mildly improve cognitive function, particularly under conditions of sleep deprivation or mental fatigue. Multiple studies show creatine supports cerebral ATP production, enhancing working memory and reaction time.

## Fact 2: Soy and Testosterone
Meta-analyses (including a 2021 meta-analysis of 41 studies) show that soy consumption does not significantly affect testosterone levels, free testosterone, or sex hormone-binding globulin in men. Phytoestrogens in soy have weak estrogenic activity insufficient to disrupt the HPG axis.

## Fact 3: Anabolic Window
Total daily protein intake matters far more than timing. The "anabolic window" is several hours wide at minimum, and muscle protein synthesis remains elevated for 24-48 hours post-exercise. A 2013 meta-analysis by Schoenfeld et al. found no significant effect of protein timing on muscle or strength gains.

## Fact 4: Seed Oils and Inflammation
Seed oils containing linoleic acid do not significantly increase inflammatory markers at normal dietary intake levels. A 2017 systematic review found that linoleic acid either had no effect on or actually reduced inflammatory markers. Major health organizations consider seed oils part of a healthy diet.

## Fact 5: Raw vs. Cooked Egg Protein
Cooking increases egg protein bioavailability from approximately 51% (raw) to 91% (cooked). Heat denatures egg proteins in a way that makes them more accessible to digestive enzymes, not less. This was established by a 1998 study in The Journal of Nutrition.

## Fact 6: Artificial Sweeteners and Insulin
Artificial sweeteners (aspartame, sucralose) cause minimal to no insulin response. While cephalic-phase insulin release is a real phenomenon, the magnitude from artificial sweeteners is negligible compared to actual sugar consumption. Multiple controlled trials confirm this.

## Fact 7: Cold Exposure and Caloric Burn
Cold exposure does activate brown adipose tissue, but the additional caloric burn is modest — approximately 50-100 extra calories per session, not 800. A 2-minute cold shower would burn even less. Cold exposure is not comparable to 60 minutes of exercise for fat loss.

## Fact 8: Ashwagandha and Testosterone
Ashwagandha (KSM-66) shows modest effects in studies — roughly 15-20% testosterone increase in some populations, primarily stressed individuals. It does not outperform testosterone replacement therapy. Effects on lean muscle mass are minimal and indirect, primarily through cortisol reduction.

## Fact 9: Collagen Supplement Absorption
Oral collagen is broken down into individual amino acids and small peptides during digestion, like any other dietary protein. Collagen peptides do not survive gastric digestion intact, do not travel through the bloodstream as whole molecules, and do not directly integrate into joint cartilage.

## Fact 10: Intermittent Fasting and Muscle Loss
16:8 intermittent fasting does not cause significant muscle loss when total daily protein intake and resistance training are maintained. Multiple studies show that IF produces similar body composition outcomes to traditional meal timing when calories and protein are matched.
